1Executive summary

Professional academies use match video every week to show players what they did and how to do it better. Grassroots players in Greater Durban almost never see themselves play. Most GDFL clubs are run by volunteers and have no video at all.

Growfit Sports Academy is a non-profit academy running U11, U13 and U15 squads, now in its third year. We are asking for R29,700 to buy an XbotGo Falcon AI sports camera and cover its first year of running costs. The camera stands on a tripod at the halfway line, follows the play on its own and records in 4K. Highlights are created automatically. There is no subscription: once the camera is bought, it costs only about R4,800 a year to run.

What the funding achieves

  • Player development: every Growfit match is filmed and reviewed by coaches. Short clips are used at Wednesday and Friday training, so 60-odd players learn from their own games.
  • Visibility for talent: players whose parents have given media consent get highlight reels, which can support high-school sport scholarships and trials.
  • Raising the level of the league: opposition clubs can buy the recording of their own match, giving other Durban clubs access to video for the first time.
  • Sustainability from the first season: a priced filming service is projected to cover the running costs 6 times over in the base case. Even the conservative case covers running costs and a fund to replace the camera.

Why a low-cost, owned camera. Premium systems such as Veo add statistics and automatic per-player reels, but they cost R70,000–R85,000 in the first year and R30,000–R45,000 every year after that. For a three-volunteer academy, a camera we own outright with almost no running costs is the safer first step. Any surplus is saved toward a premium system later (Section 8). The camera would then remain as a second camera.

How we run the academy today

Coaches mark attendance at every session, and a player who drops below 75% gets a welfare check-in. Development is tracked across five areas: technical, tactical, physical, mental and leadership. Every player's consent forms are kept on file, and the video programme uses those same consent records (Section 9), so footage follows the same safeguarding rules as the rest of the player data.

3The need

For players

Players learn faster when they see what happened. Right now, feedback after a game depends on what a coach remembers from the touchline. Video turns "keep your shape" into a clip the player can watch.

For families

High-school sports programmes and scouts increasingly ask for footage. Families in our community can't pay for private filming, so talented players stay unseen.

For the league

Most GDFL clubs have no video. A shared camera service lets the clubs we play against use their own match footage for coaching.

The barrier

The barrier is money, not interest. Subscription-based AI cameras cost a volunteer club more each year than it can raise. A camera bought once, with no subscription, removes that ongoing cost.

9Child protection, POPIA & consent

Every player we film is a child. Under POPIA (sections 34–35), children's personal information, including identifiable video, may only be processed with the consent of a parent or guardian, or under a narrow legal exception. Because Growfit stores its own footage, the academy is directly responsible for keeping it secure.

RuleHow it is applied
Consent before footage is usedGrowfit's registration pack already includes POPIA and photo/media consent. Clips and reels are only created and shared for players with consent on file, checked against the consent register before anything is shared.
Opposition playersOpposition footage goes only to the opposition club, for its own players. The club signs a short Footage Sharing Agreement confirming it holds media consent and will not publish the footage. We do not sell individual reels of opposition players.
League & venue noticeThe GDFL is informed before launch. Pre-match notice to the opponent and a sign at the venue that filming is taking place. A match is not filmed if the league or the opposition objects.
Secure storageFootage is kept in one academy-controlled cloud folder, with access limited to coaches. Memory cards are cleared after offload, and personal phones are not used for storage. Download links are private and expire.
Live streamingGrowfit's own U11–U15 league matches are not streamed publicly. A stream is only run for an event whose organiser has confirmed consent, and as an unlisted stream unless agreed otherwise.
Retention & deletionFull matches are kept for 12 months, then deleted unless needed for safeguarding. Parents can ask for their child's clips to be removed at any time.
SafeguardingCamera handled only by vetted adults (CPR Part B checked). Recording is only of matches and training, never in changing rooms. The incoming Safeguarding Officer will be the named lead for video.
Information OfficerThe Academy Director serves as Information Officer and will be registered with the Information Regulator before launch. Where the cloud storage is outside South Africa, this is disclosed in the consent form.

10Risks & mitigation

RiskMitigation
Theft or damageCamera always stays with a named adult and goes in a locked case between games. Tripod is tethered and weighted, and insurance is included in the budget. An asset register is held by the Equipment Manager, and the camera is covered by a one-year warranty.
Framing on full-size pitchesRecommended 4 m mounting height at the halfway line, tested during the pilot. Clubs are offered a free sample clip before they buy, so nobody pays for footage they can't use.
Footage lossOffloaded the same night, copy checked before the card is cleared, and a spare memory card carried on match days.
Battery on long match daysAbout 4 hours per charge, plus a power bank in the budget for back-to-back age-group games.
Low uptake by clubs or parentsThe programme's main value is coaching our own players, which doesn't depend on sales. Running costs are low enough (R4,800 a year) to be covered by one sponsor or a few filming jobs.
Volunteer capacityThe camera runs itself, so setup takes about 10 minutes. Hand-edited reels are limited to what coaches can manage. External filming jobs pay a stipend to a trained parent or youth volunteer.
Clashing fixturesOne camera can't cover games at two venues. Priority rotates fairly between age groups, published per term. A second camera is the first use of the upgrade fund.
