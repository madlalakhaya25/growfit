Seven habits of tournaments that keep their sponsors

  1. Borrow credibility. Partner with SAFA eThekwini / GDLFA, a PSL club's community trust, or a school. Sponsors fund what looks safe and well run.
  2. One title sponsor plus category partners. One headline name, then kit, hydration, equipment, media and food partners, each with a clear, exclusive role.
  3. Prizes in kind. Equipment, kit and vouchers cost sponsors less and do more for clubs than cash.
  4. Talent identification and inclusion. Scouts present, a girls' division, and bursary places all make the story easier for a sponsor to tell.
  5. Media. Community radio (Ukhozi FM), local papers (e.g. Berea Mail) and the clubs' own social pages give sponsors visibility.
  6. Use the official channel. Big brands run CSI portals with set turnaround times (the Hollywood Foundation replies within about 14 working days). Letters alone often get lost.
  7. Report back and renew. Photos, numbers and a thank-you within weeks, then the renewal conversation before budgets are set.

Every pack should include

  • The covering letter (L1–L11), personalised, with one clear ask and one smaller alternative
  • The relevant proposal (Sponsorship pack, Youth Cup, Gauteng Tour or Camps)
  • NPC registration certificate, bank confirmation letter, [PBO/NPO certificates if any], SAFA/GDLFA affiliation letter
  • 2–3 consented photos (no full names) and the Hollywoodbets kit as proof that sponsors already back us

Brand suitability

Our players are children, so every partner is checked for suitability before we accept support. We follow advertising rules that protect minors, including the gambling-advertising rules. Licensed betting partners are welcome (Hollywoodbets already supports our kit). On children's kit their branding is limited to name and logo, with no betting promotions, odds or calls to action, nothing aimed at under-18s, and no betting branding at North Crest Primary or other school premises.
