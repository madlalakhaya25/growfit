1The objective, and how we get there

"When someone in Durban football hears 'Growfit', they should think: well run, good for kids, and worth partnering with."

Our strategy is to put the Growfit brand in front of as many of the right people as possible, consistently, and to back it up with real standards. Visibility alone fades. Visibility plus proof (well-run events, safe children, prizes paid on time, partners thanked in public) builds a reputation. A reputation makes every future conversation easier.

Five rules that decide everything else

  1. The Growfit name goes on everything: kit, banners, posts, posters, letters, prizes, certificates.
  2. Show up consistently: every match, every tournament, every week online. A modest presence every week builds more reputation than one big splash.
  3. Prove our standards in public: safeguarding, consent, organised events, prizes paid on time, money reported.
  4. Make every partner look good: thank them publicly, report to them on time, and connect them with each other.
  5. Sponsors fund growth, not survival. Our own events cover the basics (Section 6), so no partner ever has to rescue us. That makes us more attractive to them.

2Positioning: who we are, in one breath

Positioning statement. Growfit Sports Academy is a Durban non-profit football academy, run in partnership with North Crest Primary School, that develops U12–U17 players on and off the pitch to a professional standard: every child plays, every child is safe, and every partner is seen.

Our story (use in every document, speech and post)

Founded in 2023 by Buhle Madlala with coaches Sphe Mlotshwa and Khaya Madlala, Growfit started with a simple belief: children from any background deserve football coached properly, safely and with ambition. Three years on, we run U12, U15 and U17 teams in the Greater Durban Football League, work with North Crest Primary School, and are building events (the Growfit Youth Cup, holiday camps and a national tour) that open doors for our players and our community.

Four message pillars

StandardsSafeguarding, consent, SAFA registration, police-cleared coaches, transparent money.
CommunityNorth Crest, families, local businesses, partner clubs. We bring people together.

How we sound

Warm, confident, proud of Durban and honest. We never exaggerate numbers, never single out a child as "needy", and always use first names only with consent.

5Marketing plan

A. Brand toolkit (build once, use everywhere)

  • Logo files (PNG, transparent, vector), colours (red, black, white), one font family
  • Canva templates: match day, result, player of the match, partner spotlight, event poster
  • Pull-up banner + a 2 × 3 m photo-wall backdrop with partner logos
  • Gazebo and flags in Growfit colours for every match and tournament
  • Email signature, letterhead, WhatsApp business profile
  • Google Business profile ("Growfit Sports Academy, Durban North") with photos and reviews
  • One link-in-bio page: registration, partner info, events, donate
  • Kit and team wear: the Growfit crest always more prominent than any partner logo

B. Social media: focus beats spread

For grassroots clubs the proven approach is two main platforms, posting 3–5 times a week, consistently. A slightly blurry photo every week beats a perfect graphic once a month. Facebook reaches parents and the community. Instagram, with reels cross-posted to TikTok, reaches players and the wider public. A WhatsApp channel reaches every family directly.

Money rules

  • Separate tracking per project (tour, Youth Cup, camps), using a reference code on every payment
  • Build a reserve of 3 months' core costs (~R19,000) before committing to new projects
  • Two people approve every payment over R2,000. Two people count all cash
  • Every partner gets a report showing how their money was spent. We publish a simple annual summary
  • Tax status confirmed with an accountant (NPC/PBO, section 18A) before promising any tax benefit

10Risks to the brand, and how we protect it

Over-promisingWe use honest, measured numbers, label targets as targets, and only offer what we can deliver every week.
A badly run eventGo/no-go checks, run of show, volunteer roster and the standards in the Youth Cup plans. Better to postpone than run a poor event.
Safeguarding incidentAppoint the Safeguarding Officer before the first Youth Cup. Consent register, police-cleared adults, a clear reporting route.
Money questionsSeparate project tracking, two signatures, reports to partners, an annual summary.
Volunteer burnoutFill the three open seats, recruit a parents' committee, and pay stipends for camps and filming jobs.
Betting partner perceptionName and logo only on children's kit, nothing at school, adult-only activations. Explain this calmly when asked.
