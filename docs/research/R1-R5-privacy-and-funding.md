# R1 + R5 — Children's data privacy (POPIA) and the SA funding environment

Desk research for Growfit (Growfit Sports Academy NPC, Greater Durban). Compiled 2026-10-06. **This is not legal or tax advice.** Labels: **FACT** = what a primary source says; **FINDING** = what a secondary source (law or audit firm, research house) reports; **INFERENCE** = our reading for Growfit, which a qualified adviser must check. "Accessed" dates are 2026-10-06 unless shown otherwise.

## R1 — POPIA and children's data

### What this means for Growfit
- **Get written consent from a parent or guardian for every player, and record it.** POPIA starts from a general ban on processing children's information (s34). For Growfit, the most practical way through it is the prior consent of a "competent person" (s35(1)(a)). The platform should store who consented, when, and to what, and should let a parent withdraw consent. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Medical information is special personal information (s26), and for a child it needs two things.** It needs a basis under s27 (consent, s27(1)(a)) and the s35 consent from a competent person. Only people with a duty of confidentiality should see it (the s32(2) model). Limit medical fields to coaches with a need to know. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Cross-border hosting is the biggest open risk.** Supabase, Vercel and Gemini may host data outside South Africa. If children's or health data goes to a foreign third party that does not give an "adequate level of protection", Growfit may need **prior authorisation** from the Regulator (s57(1)(d)) before processing. A binding agreement with each provider (a DPA whose terms closely match POPIA's 8 conditions) is the usual route to adequacy. Ask an adviser whether the vendors' standard DPAs are enough. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Gemini terms conflict with children's data:**
  - The Gemini API Additional Terms bar use in an app "directed towards or likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18".
  - On the unpaid tier, Google may use prompts to improve its products, and human reviewers may read them. Google says not to send personal information on that tier.
  - So we should (a) never use the free tier for player data, (b) keep players out of any screen that uses AI, (c) send Gemini only pseudonymised data, with no names, ID numbers or medical details, and (d) get a lawyer to look at the under-18 clause. *(INFERENCE)*
- **AI development plans must stay advisory.** s71 limits decisions with substantial effects that are based *solely* on automated profiling. A coach must review every AI plan before it is used, and no selection or exclusion decision should be automated. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Sponsors should get aggregated, de-identified numbers by default.** Examples: "42 players, 81% average attendance". POPIA defines de-identification strictly: the data must not be re-identifiable "by a reasonably foreseeable method". Small squads (U11, about 15 players) can make breakdowns re-identifiable, so suppress small cells (for example, fewer than 5). Do not share data at player level with sponsors without specific parental consent. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Photos and video need their own opt-in, separate from registration consent.** The Regulator's guidance says a child's information made public (e.g. on social media) is permitted only with a competent person's consent. Default to no names next to faces in public posts. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Set retention rules for players who leave.** s14 bans keeping records longer than necessary, then requires destruction or de-identification that "prevents its reconstruction". Set a defined retention period, then delete the record or reduce it to anonymous statistics. Keep only what law or a dispute window requires. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Register an Information Officer and plan for breaches.**
  - The Regulator's first 2026 enforcement notice (a TVET college) included failing to register an Information Officer and failing to report a security compromise.
  - Growfit should register its IO on the eServices portal and keep a written plan for notifying a breach under s22.
  - The DBE was fined R5m in 2024 for not complying with an enforcement notice, which shows the Regulator will act against bodies dealing with learners. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Marketing to parents by email or SMS needs consent.** Any electronic marketing (e.g. camps, merchandise, sponsor offers) needs consent under s69, unless the existing-customer exception applies. Every message must carry an opt-out. Operational squad messages are probably not "direct marketing", but confirm this. *(INFERENCE)*

## R5 — Sponsorship and funding environment

### What this means for Growfit
- **Sport is a small slice of CSI.** Corporate social investment (CSI) was about **R13.1bn** in 2025. Education took 44% of it; sports development took only **2%** of average spend, though 30% of companies supported sport. Soccer's share of sport CSI fell from 22% to 15%. Growfit should present itself as **youth development / education through sport**, with life skills, school attendance and safeguarding, not as football alone. *(INFERENCE)*
- **Corporates expect monitoring and evaluation (M&E).** 90% of companies measure outcomes, and 77% of NPOs do. The Grow module's attendance, milestone and retention data, aggregated, is a real advantage in funding requests. *(INFERENCE)*
- **B-BBEE Socio-Economic Development (SED)** under Statement 500 (generic codes):
  - Target: 1% of net profit after tax (NPAT).
  - Weight: 5 points.
  - At least **75% of beneficiaries must be black people**, or the claim is pro-rated.
  - Sporting development programmes are listed as a qualifying contribution.
  - Companies need a beneficiary letter: what was received, a declaration of the black beneficiary percentage, and proof of payment.
  - Growfit can produce a declaration of black beneficiary percentage from aggregate demographics. That requires collecting race data, which is **special personal information**. Collect it only with explicit consent, and keep it optional and aggregated. *(INFERENCE)*
- **No gazetted sport B-BBEE sector code was found.** Sport has a Transformation Charter, which is a different instrument. Treat the generic codes as the default. *(FINDING; verify)*
- **Section 18A is probably not available for "sport" as such.** Sport (amateur, as a pastime) is in **Part I** of the Ninth Schedule (PBO tax exemption), but it is **not listed in Part II** (the activities that can attract tax-deductible donations). A programme might qualify under Part II items such as educational enrichment for the poor and needy (3(k)) or community development (1). That depends on how the programme is designed and on SARS approval. **Do not promise donors a tax deduction until SARS has approved Growfit under s18A for a specific Part II activity.** *(INFERENCE)*
- **Sponsorship and donation are different things.** A sponsor who gets branding or marketing exposure is not making a gratuitous donation, so no 18A receipt can be issued. Growfit invoices the sponsor, and the sponsor may claim a marketing expense (VAT applies if Growfit is a VAT vendor). A donation with no return benefit can get an 18A receipt if Growfit is approved. The Grow module should keep the two separate. *(FINDING)*
- **Being an NPC is not the same as having NPO registration.** The NPC (CIPC company) must file annual returns and beneficial ownership information. NPO registration with DSD is voluntary for Growfit, but most funders require it. Once registered, Growfit must file a narrative report, annual financial statements and an accounting officer's report within 9 months of year-end, or face cancellation. PBO (s30) and 18A are separate SARS applications. *(FACT/FINDING)*
- **Use wording that never overclaims.** Use "we are a registered NPC (reg. no. …)", "NPO registration: [status]" and "we may be able to support your SED claim; please confirm eligibility with your B-BBEE verification agency". Never say "your donation is tax-deductible" or "you will earn B-BBEE points" without the approval letter and the company's own verifier's view. Every impact figure should state its period and how it was counted. *(INFERENCE)*

## Open questions for a human verifier

1. **Where is the data hosted, and is a DPA adequacy enough?** Which regions do Growfit's Supabase project, Vercel functions and Gemini endpoint use? Does each vendor's standard DPA count as a "binding agreement" giving adequate protection under s72(1)(a)? If not, must Growfit apply for prior authorisation (s57(1)(d)) before storing children's or medical data offshore?
2. **Does the Gemini under-18 clause rule Gemini out?** Does the clause ("likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18") bar Growfit's use if players have logins? Should AI features run on a different provider or under an enterprise agreement (e.g. Vertex AI)?
3. **Do ID numbers trigger prior authorisation?** Does linking SA ID numbers with SAFA/MYSAFA records amount to processing unique identifiers "for another purpose … linking with other responsible parties" under s57(1)(a)?
4. **What retention period applies?** What period is defensible for player records after a player leaves (e.g. matching the injury/claims prescription period)? Which data can be kept indefinitely once de-identified?
5. **Is Growfit already set up with the Regulator?** Is an Information Officer registered on the eServices portal, and is a PAIA manual in place?
6. **Can Growfit get 18A status?** Can it get PBO plus 18A approval for a defined Part II activity (e.g. 3(k) educational enrichment for the poor and needy, or community development)? Must the 18A-funded activity be ring-fenced from general football costs?
7. **Can donors claim SED without an 18A receipt?** Does a corporate's B-BBEE verifier need an 18A receipt for an SED claim, or will a beneficiary letter do? Will verifiers accept a pure sport programme as "income-generating", or must it include skills or employability components?
8. **How should demographic data be handled?** How should Growfit collect race or demographic data for a black beneficiary declaration lawfully: as optional special personal information, with explicit parental consent, reported only in aggregate?
9. **Does any Regulator guidance or code cover sport clubs?** Is there anything specific on photos at youth sporting events, or a code of conduct (e.g. SAFA) that would change the analysis? Nothing was found in this search.
10. **Can parent communications be sent without marketing consent?** Can operational squad messages and sponsor-funded offers to parents be split cleanly, so that s69 consent is needed only for the latter?
